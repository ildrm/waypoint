# Startup Journey Assessment — Question Bank and Recommendation Specification

Version: 1.0

Purpose: help a founder identify the next useful work on their startup journey through a limited-choice assessment and a traceable action report.

Scope: 15 stages, 30 main questions, and up to eight types of conditional follow-up. Each main question has five options except the revenue-model classifier, which has six. All selections are single-choice. This document designs the assessment; it does not administer it or claim to predict business success.

Use the most current answers for one venture, one initial customer segment, and one primary business model. Explain the relevant period and natural buying cycle when interpreting reported evidence. A skipped question is not a negative answer.

## Main questionnaire

### Stage 1: Ambition and current position

**Q01. What is your main goal for this venture?**

- **A.** A profitable business that supports my income
- **B.** A scalable company with sustainable profits
- **C.** Rapid growth with outside equity investment
- **D.** Testing an opportunity before choosing a direction
- **E.** I have not decided

**Q02. Which description best matches your venture today?**

- **A.** I am exploring problems or opportunities
- **B.** I have selected an idea but have no usable offering
- **C.** I have a prototype or pilot
- **D.** I have customers but demand is not yet consistently repeatable
- **E.** I am growing a business with repeated customer demand

### Stage 2: Customer and problem

**Q03. How clearly have you identified the initial customer?**

- **A.** A specific segment, users, buyers, and decision makers are identified
- **B.** A specific user segment is identified, but the buyer is unclear
- **C.** The target is a broad market
- **D.** No initial customer segment has been selected
- **E.** I am not sure

**Q04. What is your strongest evidence that the problem matters?**

- **A.** Repeated observation or records show the problem and its consequences
- **B.** Several target customers described recent examples and workarounds
- **C.** Informal opinions or general complaints
- **D.** My own assumptions or experience only
- **E.** No evidence has been gathered

### Stage 3: Market and alternatives

**Q05. How thoroughly have you investigated existing alternatives?**

- **A.** Direct competitors, indirect alternatives, and doing nothing are mapped
- **B.** Direct competitors have been compared
- **C.** I know some competitor names but have not compared them
- **D.** No alternatives have been investigated
- **E.** I am not sure

**Q06. How can you reach your first suitable customers?**

- **A.** Named prospects and routes to their decision makers are identified
- **B.** A relevant audience or prospect source is identified but untested
- **C.** I only have broad market statistics
- **D.** I cannot yet identify a practical route to customers
- **E.** This has not been assessed

### Stage 4: Value proposition and differentiation

**Q07. How clearly is the customer outcome demonstrated?**

- **A.** Real customers have achieved a measurable improvement
- **B.** A measurable target is defined but has not been demonstrated
- **C.** The offer is mainly described through features
- **D.** The customer outcome is unclear
- **E.** I am not sure

**Q08. What supports your reason for customers to choose you?**

- **A.** Customers have chosen us and explained the specific reason
- **B.** A specific advantage is proposed but not tested
- **C.** The main proposed advantage is a lower price, not yet tested
- **D.** No clear reason to switch or buy is defined
- **E.** This has not been assessed

### Stage 5: Revenue model and pricing

**Q09. What is your primary intended revenue model?**

- **A.** One-time product sales or fixed projects
- **B.** Subscriptions or ongoing service contracts
- **C.** Marketplace commissions
- **D.** Usage-based or transaction fees for our own service
- **E.** Advertising or sponsorship
- **F.** Not yet decided

**Q10. What is your strongest evidence for the intended price?**

- **A.** Independent target customers have paid the intended normal price
- **B.** Customers have paid a discounted or introductory price only
- **C.** Prospects have expressed interest without paying
- **D.** The price was selected internally without customer testing
- **E.** No price has been proposed

### Stage 6: Demand evidence and experimentation

**Q11. What is the strongest commercial commitment achieved?**

- **A.** Multiple independent customers have completed purchases, with repeat purchases where applicable
- **B.** At least one independent customer has completed a purchase
- **C.** There are paid pilots, deposits, or orders awaiting delivery
- **D.** There are only unpaid signups, promises, or expressions of interest
- **E.** There are no customer commitments yet

**Q12. How do you test important business assumptions?**

- **A.** We record hypotheses, measures, and decision rules before tests
- **B.** We run tests but mainly decide afterward how to interpret them
- **C.** We collect feedback without structured experiments
- **D.** We build or launch mainly on intuition
- **E.** No testing approach has been chosen

### Stage 7: People and responsibilities

**Q13. How are the essential responsibilities covered?**

- **A.** All critical responsibilities have capable named owners
- **B.** One important capability gap is known
- **C.** Several important capabilities are missing
- **D.** Responsibilities have not been mapped
- **E.** I am not sure

**Q14. How clear are ownership, roles, and decision rights among founders?**

- **A.** Written agreements exist and work in practice
- **B.** There is informal agreement but little documentation
- **C.** Important points are unclear or have not been discussed
- **D.** There is an unresolved disagreement
- **E.** I am the only founder

### Stage 8: Feasibility and operating requirements

**Q15. How far has delivery feasibility been demonstrated?**

- **A.** The full delivery process has worked repeatedly in realistic conditions
- **B.** A realistic feasibility test has worked, with remaining uncertainties
- **C.** Only an internal concept or demonstration exists
- **D.** A critical technical, supply, or operating obstacle is unresolved
- **E.** Feasibility has not been assessed

**Q16. How well do you understand requirements for operating in your intended market?**

- **A.** Applicable requirements were checked and current prerequisites are addressed
- **B.** A review is underway and requirements remain open
- **C.** Requirements have not been checked
- **D.** A known requirement currently prevents the planned activity
- **E.** I am not sure

### Stage 9: Minimum viable offering

**Q17. What can a customer use today?**

- **A.** A functioning offering is used in regular customer delivery
- **B.** A limited usable pilot is available
- **C.** Only a mockup, sample concept, or internal demonstration exists
- **D.** Only an idea or written description exists
- **E.** The offering is currently being redesigned

**Q18. What happens when customers try the core offering?**

- **A.** They consistently achieve the intended outcome
- **B.** Some achieve the outcome, while others struggle
- **C.** The outcome has not yet been evaluated with external customers
- **D.** Most tested customers do not achieve the intended outcome
- **E.** Results have not been measured

### Stage 10: Acquisition and sales

**Q19. Which primary channel currently brings paying customers?**

- **A.** Founder outreach or direct sales
- **B.** Search, content, or paid digital campaigns
- **C.** Referrals or partners
- **D.** Retail, events, distribution, or external marketplaces
- **E.** No channel has brought paying customers yet

**Q20. How repeatable is customer acquisition?**

- **A.** A channel has repeatedly produced customers with acceptable measured acquisition costs
- **B.** A channel has produced customers, but costs or consistency are uncertain
- **C.** Customers have arrived mainly through personal relationships or exceptional deals
- **D.** There are no completed customer acquisitions yet
- **E.** Acquisition performance has not been measured

### Stage 11: Economics and cash

**Q21. After all variable delivery costs, what remains from a typical sale?**

- **A.** A positive contribution supported by actual delivery records
- **B.** It appears positive, but some variable costs are missing
- **C.** Zero or a negative contribution
- **D.** No customer delivery has been completed yet
- **E.** Contribution has not been calculated

**Q22. How long can available cash support the current operating plan?**

- **A.** Operations generate enough cash for current commitments without new funding
- **B.** More than 12 months
- **C.** At least 3 months and up to 12 months
- **D.** Less than 3 months
- **E.** A cash forecast has not been prepared

### Stage 12: Retention and customer pull

**Q23. At the next relevant use or buying opportunity, do users or customers return?**

- **A.** Most eligible groups consistently return or renew
- **B.** Some return, but the pattern is mixed
- **C.** Most eligible users or customers do not return
- **D.** The relevant use, buying, or renewal interval has not occurred yet
- **E.** Return behavior has not been measured

**Q24. What evidence exists of customers voluntarily bringing more business?**

- **A.** Repeated unsolicited referrals or voluntary account expansion
- **B.** Some unsolicited referrals or voluntary expansion
- **C.** Referrals occur mainly after incentives or repeated requests
- **D.** None has been observed despite enough customer activity to assess it
- **E.** There is not enough customer activity or measurement to judge

### Stage 13: Funding for the next milestone

**Q25. Are resources for the next defined milestone available?**

- **A.** Yes; its scope, cost, and resources are documented and covered
- **B.** Only part of the estimated resources is covered
- **C.** The milestone is defined but resources are not covered
- **D.** The milestone or its cost has not been defined
- **E.** I am not sure

**Q26. Which funding source would you prefer to evaluate next?**

- **A.** Customer revenue or founder funds
- **B.** Grants or other non-dilutive support
- **C.** Loans or other debt
- **D.** Equity investors
- **E.** I need help comparing suitable options

### Stage 14: Capacity and controlled expansion

**Q27. How consistently can the core operation deliver?**

- **A.** Documented processes deliver consistent quality with clear owners
- **B.** Delivery is repeatable but relies heavily on particular people
- **C.** Quality or timing deteriorates as volume increases
- **D.** Delivery has not yet been repeated enough to assess consistency
- **E.** Consistency has not been measured

**Q28. What is the main constraint on current growth?**

- **A.** Demand is available, but delivery capacity limits us
- **B.** Delivery capacity is available, but acquiring customers limits us
- **C.** Cash or per-sale economics limits us
- **D.** Several constraints exist and the main one is unclear
- **E.** It is too early to evaluate growth constraints

### Stage 15: Dependencies and learning

**Q29. How exposed is the venture to a critical dependency?**

- **A.** Major dependencies are mapped and practical fallbacks exist
- **B.** One major dependency is known but has no workable fallback
- **C.** Several major dependencies have no workable fallbacks
- **D.** Dependencies have not been assessed
- **E.** No critical dependency has been identified after review

**Q30. How are results used to change business decisions?**

- **A.** Regular reviews use current evidence and recorded continue/change/stop decisions
- **B.** Regular reviews occur, but decision rules are informal
- **C.** Reviews mainly follow setbacks or urgent problems
- **D.** There is no regular review process
- **E.** The venture is too early for a review history

## Conditional questions

### C01

**Show when:** Q09=A. Ask immediately after Q09, before Q23.

**Question:** How often can the same customer naturally need this offering?

- **A.** Regularly or through replenishment
- **B.** Occasionally, when a new need arises
- **C.** It is normally a single lifetime purchase
- **D.** The pattern is unknown

**Routing and recommendation:** For C, skip Q23 as not applicable; assess fit using outcomes, paid demand, acquisition economics, and relevant referrals. For B, measure return only when a new need occurs. For D, retention is unknown; do not assume recurring demand.

### C02

**Show when:** Q16=B/C/D/E.

**Question:** Could the planned activity involve sector licensing, sensitive personal data, or physical safety requirements?

- **A.** Yes
- **B.** No, based on an initial review
- **C.** I do not know

**Routing and recommendation:** For A/C, clarify applicable requirements with suitable local expertise before the affected activity. A known blocking requirement in Q16=D stays a blocker until resolved. Do not infer a jurisdiction or assert permission from this answer.

### C03

**Show when:** Q22=D/E.

**Question:** Can available cash cover already committed payments falling due in the next 30 days?

- **A.** Yes, according to a cash forecast
- **B.** Only some committed payments are covered
- **C.** No
- **D.** There are no committed payments in that period
- **E.** I do not know

**Routing and recommendation:** For B/C, prioritize cash forecasting, collections, reviewing discretionary commitments, and an explicit financing or scope decision. For E, establish the forecast first. Do not automatically recommend debt, layoffs, or investor fundraising.

### C04

**Show when:** Q09=C.

**Question:** What is the strongest evidence that the marketplace can complete exchanges?

- **A.** Both sides repeatedly match and complete transactions
- **B.** Supply is available, but demand is insufficient
- **C.** Demand is available, but supply is insufficient
- **D.** Neither side is sufficiently active
- **E.** This has not been tested

**Routing and recommendation:** For B/C, focus on the constrained side in one narrow segment or geography. For D/E, test a manually coordinated match before broad recruitment. Analyze completion rates, repeat participation, take rate, and contribution; registrations alone are insufficient.

### C05

**Show when:** Q09=E.

**Question:** Which evidence exists for an advertising or sponsorship model?

- **A.** A returning audience and paying advertisers or sponsors
- **B.** A returning audience, but no paying advertisers or sponsors
- **C.** Paying advertisers or sponsors, but audience return is unproven
- **D.** Neither side has demonstrated demand
- **E.** This has not been measured

**Routing and recommendation:** Evaluate audience behavior and advertiser/sponsor outcomes separately. Q23 concerns audience return; Q10 and Q11 concern paying advertisers/sponsors. Do not infer commercial fit from audience size alone.

### C06

**Show when:** Q13=B/C.

**Question:** Which capability gap most constrains the next milestone?

- **A.** Product or service delivery
- **B.** Sales or customer acquisition
- **C.** Domain expertise, supply, or operations
- **D.** Financial or administrative management
- **E.** The main gap is unclear

**Routing and recommendation:** Attach the relevant responsibility to the next milestone. Consider a founder, collaborator, partner, or scoped external help; do not automatically recommend a permanent hire or cofounder.

### C07

**Show when:** A contradiction is detected between answers describing the same offering, segment, and period. Repeat only for material conflicts.

**Question:** These two answers appear inconsistent: [show the exact questions and selected answers]. Which explanation applies?

- **A.** The earlier answer reflects the current situation; the later answer needs updating
- **B.** The later answer reflects the current situation; the earlier answer needs updating
- **C.** Both are true for different segments or offerings
- **D.** Neither answer is current
- **E.** I am not sure

**Routing and recommendation:** For A/B/D, reopen affected questions and preserve revisions. For C, separate the assessment by segment or offering; do not combine metrics. For E, keep the conflict visible, lower confidence in related conclusions, and avoid unsupported maturity labels.

### C08

**Show when:** Q18=B/D or Q23=C, unless Q23 was skipped.

**Question:** What appears to be the main reason customers do not achieve the outcome or return?

- **A.** They struggle to start or understand the offering
- **B.** The offering does not solve the core problem well enough
- **C.** Price or total customer cost prevents continued use
- **D.** Delivery, reliability, or service quality causes problems
- **E.** The reason has not been established

**Routing and recommendation:** Treat A-D as hypotheses until checked against behavior and interviews. Diagnose the dominant cause and test one focused change; for E, investigate before adding features or increasing acquisition spend.

## Recommendation logic

This is a proposed qualitative decision aid, not a statistically validated instrument. It does not estimate startup success probability. Options are not globally ordered: A is not always better, and goals, funding preferences, and revenue models receive no maturity score.

### Processing order

1. Preserve exact question text, option IDs, selected labels, dates, questionnaire version, and answer revisions.
2. Apply C01-C08 routing. Record skipped questions as `not_applicable` with a reason; keep `unknown`, `not_yet_observable`, and `unanswered` separate.
3. Detect material contradictions; use C07 and reopen affected answers. A change to Q09 invalidates any now-irrelevant branch answers and triggers the appropriate new branch. Recalculate dependent recommendations after edits.
4. Evaluate constraints before growth recommendations: known unmet operating prerequisites (Q16=D), near-term committed-payment shortfalls (C03=B/C), critical unresolved feasibility obstacles (Q15=D), and unresolved founder disputes (Q14=D). State which activity each constraint affects.
5. If delivered outcomes are weak (Q18=D), contribution is nonpositive (Q21=C), eligible retention is poor (Q23=C), or delivery deteriorates with volume (Q27=C), prioritize diagnosis before broad expansion. A limited, deliberately budgeted experiment can still be appropriate.
6. Address foundational unknowns. Do not call unmeasured contribution negative, young cohorts churned, nonpaying interest revenue, or insufficient evidence a proven failure.
7. Select no more than three primary actions. Rank by immediacy of consequences, dependency on earlier work, and fit with the founder's goal. Group duplicate actions. Keep other issues in a later-work list.
8. Explain each action using the exact triggering answers and an observable next decision. Keep rules deterministic; any generated prose must stay within these inputs.

### Suggested current focus

These labels describe work to prioritize, not a definitive company valuation or proof of product-market fit. A constraint can override the normal sequence.

- **Customer discovery:** Q03=C/D/E or Q04=C/D/E. Specify the missing customer or problem evidence.
- **Solution testing:** customer/problem evidence exists, but realistic delivery or external outcomes are not yet demonstrated (for example Q15=C/E, Q17=C/D, or Q18=C/E).
- **Commercial validation:** a usable result exists, but normal-price demand, acquisition, contribution, or appropriate repeat-use evidence remains incomplete.
- **Operating improvement:** customers exist, but outcome quality, return behavior, delivery consistency, contribution, or dependencies require attention.
- **Controlled expansion assessment:** consider only when Q03=A/B, Q04=A/B, Q07=A, Q10=A, Q11=A, Q15=A, Q16=A, Q18=A, Q20=A, Q21=A, Q27=A/B, and relevant retention/marketplace/advertising evidence supports the model. Q22 and Q25 must also show the proposed expansion is fundable. These conservative gates are product heuristics, not universal scientific thresholds; independently verify the underlying records before a major commitment.
- **Insufficient evidence:** use where the relevant branch is incomplete or unresolved conflicts prevent a reliable interpretation. State exactly which information is missing.

### Dimension status

Show a separate status for customer/problem, market access, value/pricing, demand, feasibility, delivery, economics, cash, retention, team, and operating capacity. Use four labels:

- **Supported by reported evidence:** the user describes relevant repeated behavior or records. This is self-reported unless independently verified.
- **Being tested:** a defined hypothesis or limited test exists.
- **Known issue:** the answer explicitly reports an obstacle or poor observed result.
- **Unknown / not yet observable:** no suitable measurement exists, or the relevant interval has not occurred.

Show not-applicable items separately. A strong dimension must not cancel out an immediate cash or operating constraint. Do not average the dimensions into a single startup success score.

### Metrics by model

| Model | Metrics to suggest where relevant |
| --- | --- |
| One-time product or project | Paid conversion, delivered customer outcome, contribution, acquisition cost, returns/rework, referrals, and repeat buying only when a natural new need occurs |
| Subscription or ongoing service | Activation, outcome achieved, cohort retention, renewal, churn, contribution, acquisition cost, and observed payback |
| Marketplace | Activity on both sides, successful matches, transaction completion, repeat participation, take rate, contribution, and concentration |
| Usage or transaction fees | Successful completed usage, frequency at natural need intervals, reliability, net fees, variable usage costs, and contribution |
| Advertising or sponsorship | Returning audience, relevant engagement, paying advertiser/sponsor demand, advertiser outcomes or renewal, and revenue less audience/delivery/acquisition costs |

Do not invent a universal retention percentage, customer count, acquisition ratio, or payback threshold. Let the venture define a test criterion appropriate to its model, buying cycle, resources, and available evidence. Do not estimate lifetime value from a cohort too young to support the estimate.

## Final report specification

The report must be understandable without reopening the assessment.

1. **Profile:** main goal, self-described stage, primary revenue model, current offering, team situation, cash position, and funding preference. Use only selected answers.
2. **Suggested focus:** the most relevant work stage, with the supporting answers and any uncertainty. Distinguish self-described maturity from evidence.
3. **Full question-and-answer record:** include every presented question and exact selected label, grouped by stage. Include conditional questions. Mark skipped, unanswered, and revised items distinctly.
4. **Interpretation:** for each stage, explain what the answers support, what remains uncertain, and the practical implication.
5. **Strengths:** up to three supported strengths; do not manufacture strengths if the evidence is missing.
6. **Priority issues:** up to three, with severity, affected activity, answer references, and why each matters.
7. **Action plan:** for each priority give an action, suggested accountable role, first deliverable, metric or evidence to collect, and a continue/change/stop review criterion. Any proposed numeric target must be labeled a proposed experiment target, never an industry standard.
8. **30/60/90-day draft:** sequence the next experiments and operating actions; explain dependencies. These are planning windows and must be adjusted for approval cycles, buying intervals, physical production, and available time/cash.
9. **Financing and expansion guidance:** align with goals and evidence; do not prescribe equity, debt, hiring, or international expansion solely from a preference.
10. **Later work and review trigger:** retain lower-priority actions and define the next meaningful reassessment event.
11. **Evidence limits:** identify that answers are self-reported, any unresolved inconsistencies, and any untested assumptions. Never supply a success probability or imply a legal, investment, or funding eligibility determination.

## Interaction requirements

- Display one question at a time with single-select options and no preselected answer.
- Show stage progress based on the active branch; recompute the total if the branch changes.
- Allow Back, review, answer editing, and save/resume. Never silently overwrite prior selections.
- Explain terms on demand: independent customer, normal price, contribution, cash runway, cohort, and variable cost.
- Tell users to select the closest accurate option and use uncertainty choices when needed. “Most” or “repeated” must be supported by a denominator, natural opportunity, and records before major decisions; this quick assessment does not collect that verification.
- For channel and funding questions, select the primary current channel or preferred next funding category. Preferences can be revisited.
- Do not display per-answer judgments during the questionnaire; present interpretations after submission.
- For pre-revenue ventures, use not-yet-observable responses instead of inferring poor retention. Skip Q23 only when C01 establishes that repeat purchase or use is structurally inapplicable; preserve the skip reason. Q27-Q28 remain available so the user can accurately report that repetition or growth cannot yet be assessed.
- A user may finish an incomplete assessment; the report must visibly describe missing evidence and avoid conclusions that depend on unanswered questions.
- For advertised/free-user models, audience use and paying-customer evidence are separate. If more than one material revenue model or customer segment exists, assess each separately instead of blending them.
- Use plain language, keyboard-accessible controls, visible focus, readable contrast, and a review screen before report generation.
- Store and reuse answers only in accordance with the product's disclosed data choices. Do not treat financial answers as permission to initiate transactions or contact third parties.

**Profile:** You aim to build a scalable profitable company. You have a usable pilot and interview evidence, but external outcomes, normal-price demand, and acquisition have not yet been demonstrated. You prefer customer revenue or founder funds, and report 3-12 months of runway under the current plan.

**Suggested focus:** Solution testing followed by commercial validation. The pilot exists, but Q18=C means its customer outcome is still untested; Q10=C and Q11=D describe interest rather than completed paid demand.

**Strengths:** A specific customer segment and buying roles are identified (Q03=A), named prospects are accessible (Q06=A), and a realistic feasibility test has worked (Q15=B). None of these alone proves product-market fit.

**Three priorities:**

1. **Prepare a suitable external pilot.** Clarify operating requirements before any affected activity (Q16=C, C02=C), define one measurable customer outcome (Q07=B), and identify a named owner for sales (Q13=B, C06=B). Deliverable: a pilot brief with prerequisites, scope, participant criteria, and a proposed decision rule.
2. **Test outcome and price with independent prospects.** Use the accessible prospect list (Q06=A), offer a clearly scoped pilot, and distinguish unpaid feedback from payment (Q10=C, Q11=D). Deliverable: observed outcomes, actual customer decisions, objections, and a record of what was promised and delivered.
3. **Complete contribution and cash planning before larger commitments.** Contribution is uncalculated (Q21=E), the next milestone is only partly resourced (Q25=B), and a major dependency lacks a fallback (Q29=B). Deliverable: a cost model, milestone cash forecast, and a practical fallback for the most consequential dependency.

**First 30 days:** clarify prerequisites, assign responsibilities, define a transparent offer and experiment, and begin an appropriately scoped external pilot.

**Days 31-60:** measure delivered outcomes, test the proposed normal price where appropriate, and calculate actual delivery contribution. Improve the core result if customers struggle.

**Days 61-90:** attempt to reproduce the result with additional independent customers and measure acquisition effort. Consider a controlled increase in volume only if delivery, contribution, cash, and demand evidence support it; otherwise revise the relevant hypothesis.

**Review criterion:** after the first complete pilot cycle, review actual customer outcomes, purchase decisions, and delivery costs. Set numeric experiment targets before the test rather than presenting invented benchmarks as facts.

**Report limits:** all evidence is hypothetical and self-reported. Pricing interest has not been treated as revenue; immature retention has not been treated as churn.
